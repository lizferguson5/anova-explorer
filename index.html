BIOL 200 · One-way ANOVA: where the variation comes from (between, within and residual), plus a worked antibiotic-trial example.

The same antibiotic trial as tab ②: 18 patients and three antibiotics. Factor variable: antibiotic (A, B, C). Response variable: number of days until the infection cleared. Patients don't all recover in the same number of days. ANOVA asks where that variation comes from and splits it into two sources.
Total variation
Each patient's distance from the grand mean (all patients together).
SS(T) = SS(B) + SS(W)
Between groups
Each group's mean compared with the grand mean: the part explained by which antibiotic a patient got.
Also called: treatment · among-groups · signal
SS(B)
Within groups
Each patient compared with their own group's mean: differences the antibiotic can't explain.
Also called: residual · error · noise
SS(W)

Which antibiotic clears the infection fastest?

Patients with a bacterial skin infection were randomly assigned to one of three antibiotics. Researchers recorded the number of days until each patient's infection cleared (fewer days = better). A few patients dropped out, so the groups are different sizes.

Is the mean number of days to recovery the same for all three antibiotics?

Factor: antibiotic (3 groups) · Response: days until infection cleared · H₀: μA = μB = μC · Hₐ: at least one mean is different
